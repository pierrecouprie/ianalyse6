Audio descriptors — formulas

This page describes the audio descriptors computed by iAnalyse. The formulas use the following notation:

SymbolMeaning
x[n]Time-domain sample in the analysis frame
NFrame size
X[k]Linear magnitude of FFT bin k
f_kCenter frequency of bin k, between 0 and Nyquist
L[n], R[n]Left and right channel samples
epsSmall constant that avoids log(0) or unstable division

General pipeline​

Audio files are converted through the current analysis pipeline, then descriptors are computed frame by frame. Time-domain descriptors use the frame’s signal directly. Spectral descriptors use a dedicated FFT that returns linear magnitudes, separate from the FFT used to display the spectrogram. Differential descriptors compare the current frame with the previous frame.

Typical pipeline:

Audio -> resampling 44100 Hz -> decoupage en fenetres -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> descripteurs spectraux

For stereo descriptors:

Audio stereo -> extraction des deux premiers canaux -> decoupage synchronise -> calcul L/R ou MID/SIDE

iAnalyse data graphs use a simplified display scale. Some descriptors naturally fall within 0...1; others use a maxRange or minRange for display.

Peak Frequency​

How it works​

Peak Frequency finds the spectral bin with the highest magnitude and returns its center frequency. It therefore represents the dominant frequency in the frame, without sub-bin interpolation or perceptual pitch estimation.

Formula​

kmax=arg⁡max⁡kX[k]k_{max} = \arg\max_k X[k]kmax​=argkmax​X[k] Fpeak=fkmaxF_{peak} = f_{k_{max}}Fpeak​=fkmax​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> recherche du maximum -> frequence du bin

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to follow a dominant frequency, resonance, prominent fundamental or stable spectral peak. It is less reliable for noisy textures, dense chords or sounds whose fundamental is not the strongest peak.

RMS Amplitude​

How it works​

RMS Amplitude measures the average signal energy in the frame. Unlike peak amplitude, RMS better represents local average power.

Formula​

RMS=1N∑n=0N−1x[n]2RMS = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]^2}RMS=N1​n=0∑N−1​x[n]2​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> carre des echantillons -> moyenne -> racine carree

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to follow dynamics, crescendos, energy changes and relatively silent regions. It also underlies other measures such as Loudness and Onset Strength.

Spectral Centroid​

How it works​

Spectral Centroid computes the spectrum’s frequency center of gravity. Higher values indicate that energy is concentrated at higher frequencies.

Formula​

C=∑kfkX[k]∑kX[k]C = \frac{\sum_k f_k X[k]}{\sum_k X[k]}C=∑k​X[k]∑k​fk​X[k]​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> ponderation par frequence -> moyenne ponderee

Use in musical analysis​

It is useful for tracking brightness, filtering, timbral changes and the overall evolution of the spectrum. In electroacoustic music, it can clearly reveal transitions between dark and bright sounds.

Spectral Crest​

How it works​

Spectral Crest measures the ratio of the spectral maximum to the spectral mean. The calculation excludes the DC bin so that DC or very low-frequency energy does not bias the result.

Formula​

SC=max⁡k>0X[k]1K−1∑k=1K−1X[k]SC = \frac{\max_{k>0} X[k]}{\frac{1}{K-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}X[k]}SC=K−11​∑k=1K−1​X[k]maxk>0​X[k]​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> suppression DC -> maximum / moyenne

Use in musical analysis​

It distinguishes a spectrum with a dominant peak from one with more evenly distributed energy. Use it to distinguish pitched sounds and resonances from more diffuse spectral masses.

Spectral Decrease​

How it works​

Spectral Decrease measures the overall decline in spectral magnitudes relative to the first bin. A pronounced value indicates a steep decrease in energy with frequency.

Formula​

SD=∑k=1K−1X[k]−X[0]k∑k=1K−1X[k]SD = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\frac{X[k]-X[0]}{k}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}X[k]}SD=∑k=1K−1​X[k]∑k=1K−1​kX[k]−X[0]​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> comparaison au premier bin -> normalisation par energie hors DC

Use in musical analysis​

It can help identify dark, damped spectra or spectra concentrated in the bass. It is also useful for observing the evolution of low-pass filtering or spectral decay.

Spectral Flatness​

How it works​

Spectral Flatness compares the geometric and arithmetic means of the spectrum. A value near 1 indicates a flat, noise-like spectrum; a value near 0 indicates a more tonal or peaked spectrum.

Formula​

SF=exp⁡(1K∑klog⁡(X[k]+ϵ))1K∑kX[k]SF = \frac{\exp\left(\frac{1}{K}\sum_k \log(X[k] + \epsilon)\right)} {\frac{1}{K}\sum_k X[k]}SF=K1​∑k​X[k]exp(K1​∑k​log(X[k]+ϵ))​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> moyenne geometrique / moyenne arithmetique

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to distinguish noisy, breathy or granular sounds from harmonic or resonant sounds. It can also support segmentation by texture type.

Spectral Flux​

How it works​

Spectral Flux measures the distance between two consecutive spectra. It accounts for both increases and decreases in spectral energy.

Formula​

Fluxt=∑k(Xt[k]−Xt−1[k])2Flux_t = \sqrt{\sum_k (X_t[k] - X_{t-1}[k])^2}Fluxt​=k∑​(Xt​[k]−Xt−1​[k])2​

Pipeline​

Fenetre precedente et fenetre courante -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> difference bin a bin -> norme euclidienne

Use in musical analysis​

It is useful for detecting spectral changes, texture boundaries, transitions and some event onsets. It is often relevant to a future segmentation assistance interface.

Spectral Rolloff​

How it works​

Spectral Rolloff finds the frequency below which a specified percentage of cumulative spectral energy lies. The current threshold comes from the settings, often around 85 or 90 percent.

Formula​

R=fmtel que∑k=0mX[k]≥p∑k=0K−1X[k]R = f_m \quad \text{tel que} \quad \sum_{k=0}^{m} X[k] \geq p \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} X[k]R=fm​tel quek=0∑m​X[k]≥pk=0∑K−1​X[k]

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre d'analyse -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> somme cumulee -> frequence au seuil

Use in musical analysis​

It indicates the effective spectral extent. Use it to track spectral expansion, filtering, brightness and transitions between low sounds and sounds rich in high frequencies.

Spectral Standard Deviation​

How it works​

Spectral Standard Deviation measures frequency dispersion around the spectral centroid. It is the square root of spectral variance.

Formula​

σ=∑k(fk−C)2X[k]∑kX[k]\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_k (f_k - C)^2 X[k]}{\sum_k X[k]}}σ=∑k​X[k]∑k​(fk​−C)2X[k]​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> centroide spectral -> variance ponderee -> racine carree

Use in musical analysis​

It helps distinguish concentrated spectra from broad spectra. It can reveal changes in spectral width independently of overall level.

Spectral Variance​

How it works​

Spectral Variance measures the squared dispersion of the spectrum around the centroid. A high value indicates energy spread far from the spectral center.

Formula​

σ2=∑k(fk−C)2X[k]∑kX[k]\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_k (f_k - C)^2 X[k]}{\sum_k X[k]}σ2=∑k​X[k]∑k​(fk​−C)2X[k]​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> centroide spectral -> dispersion quadratique ponderee

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to observe overall spectral width. It can help compare narrow sounds, such as whistles or resonances, with broader or noisier sounds.

Zero Crossing Rate​

How it works​

Zero Crossing Rate counts sign changes in the time-domain frame. The result is normalized by the number of possible transitions.

Formula​

ZCR=12(N−1)∑n=1N−1∣sgn⁡(x[n])−sgn⁡(x[n−1])∣ZCR = \frac{1}{2(N-1)}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1} |\operatorname{sgn}(x[n]) - \operatorname{sgn}(x[n-1])|ZCR=2(N−1)1​n=1∑N−1​∣sgn(x[n])−sgn(x[n−1])∣

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> signes successifs -> comptage des passages par zero

Use in musical analysis​

It is sensitive to high frequencies, noisy textures and unstable signals. It can help distinguish low, stable sounds from noisy sounds or sounds rich in high frequencies.

Average Deviation​

How it works​

Average Deviation measures the mean absolute deviation of samples from their mean. In iAnalyse, it is computed on the time-domain signal.

Formula​

AD=1N∑n=0N−1∣x[n]−μ∣AD = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}|x[n] - \mu|AD=N1​n=0∑N−1​∣x[n]−μ∣ μ=1N∑n=0N−1x[n]\mu = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]μ=N1​n=0∑N−1​x[n]

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> moyenne -> ecart absolu moyen

Use in musical analysis​

It provides a simple indication of amplitude variability within a frame. It can complement RMS to distinguish regular frames from more fluctuating ones, but it has not been aligned with the LibXtract/Sonic Visualiser output.

Inharmonicity​

How it works​

Inharmonicity estimates how far the spectrum deviates from multiples of an approximate fundamental. The fundamental is estimated from the strongest non-DC spectral bin. Each bin then contributes its distance from the nearest harmonic multiple, weighted by squared amplitude.

Formula​

hk=⌊fkf0+0.5⌋h_k = \left\lfloor \frac{f_k}{f_0} + 0.5 \right\rfloorhk​=⌊f0​fk​​+0.5⌋ I=2∑k>0∣fk−hkf0∣X[k]2f0∑k>0X[k]2I = \frac{2 \sum_{k>0}|f_k - h_k f_0| X[k]^2} {f_0 \sum_{k>0} X[k]^2}I=f0​∑k>0​X[k]22∑k>0​∣fk​−hk​f0​∣X[k]2​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> estimation f0 par pic maximal -> ecart aux harmoniques -> moyenne ponderee

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to observe a sound’s degree of inharmonicity. It is relevant to distinguishing harmonic sounds, bells, metallic percussion, complex textures and timbral transformations.

Spectral Kurtosis​

How it works​

Spectral Kurtosis measures the concentration or peakedness of the spectral distribution around the centroid. A high value indicates a distribution with pronounced peaks or tails.

Formula​

Kurt=∑k(fk−C)4X[k]∑kX[k]σ4Kurt = \frac{\frac{\sum_k (f_k - C)^4 X[k]}{\sum_k X[k]}}{\sigma^4}Kurt=σ4∑k​X[k]∑k​(fk​−C)4X[k]​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> centroide -> variance -> moment d'ordre 4 normalise

Use in musical analysis​

It helps characterize highly concentrated spectra, resonances or sounds with a few dominant components. It can complement variance and crest to describe spectral shape.

Loudness​

How it works​

Loudness is a simple perceptual approximation based on RMS. It applies power-law compression to bring the measure closer to perceived level.

Formula​

Loudness=RMS0.6Loudness = RMS^{0.6}Loudness=RMS0.6

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> RMS -> compression par puissance

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to follow perceived dynamics rather than strictly linear energy. It can help visualize changes in intensity or dynamic contrasts.

Noisiness​

How it works​

Noisiness is a proxy derived from Harmonic Ratio. The less periodic the signal, the greater its noisiness.

Formula​

Noisiness=1−HarmonicRatioNoisiness = 1 - HarmonicRatioNoisiness=1−HarmonicRatio

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> harmonic ratio par autocorrelation -> complement a 1

Use in musical analysis​

It can help track the noisy or unstable component of a signal. It is useful for distinguishing noisy textures from periodic sounds, but remains an experimental proxy.

Spectral Skewness​

How it works​

Spectral Skewness measures the asymmetry of the spectral distribution around the centroid. A positive value indicates a distribution extending more toward high frequencies; a negative value indicates an extension toward low frequencies.

Formula​

Skew=∑k(fk−C)3X[k]∑kX[k]σ3Skew = \frac{\frac{\sum_k (f_k - C)^3 X[k]}{\sum_k X[k]}}{\sigma^3}Skew=σ3∑k​X[k]∑k​(fk​−C)3X[k]​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> centroide -> ecart-type -> moment d'ordre 3 normalise

Use in musical analysis​

It helps reveal which side of the centroid the spectral energy extends toward. It can be useful for observing timbral shifts or asymmetric filtering.

Spectral Sharpness​

How it works​

Spectral Sharpness estimates perceptual brightness by giving greater weight to high frequencies. The current version is a direct approximation over FFT bins, rather than a full psychoacoustic Bark-band decomposition.

Formula​

Sharpness=∑kX[k]⋅Fk⋅w(Fk)∑kX[k]Sharpness = \frac{\sum_k X[k] \cdot F_k \cdot w(F_k)} {\sum_k X[k]}Sharpness=∑k​X[k]∑k​X[k]⋅Fk​⋅w(Fk​)​ Fk=fk1000F_k = \frac{f_k}{1000}Fk​=1000fk​​ w(Fk)={1si Fk≤1Fksinonw(F_k) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{si } F_k \leq 1 \\ F_k & \text{sinon} \end{cases}w(Fk​)={1Fk​​si Fk​≤1sinon​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> conversion frequence en kHz -> ponderation hautes frequences -> moyenne ponderee

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to track brightness, high-frequency attacks, filtering and changes in timbral color. It is especially useful for sounds whose perception strongly depends on high-frequency energy.

Spectral Slope​

How it works​

Spectral Slope estimates the linear slope of the spectrum on a frequency axis normalized between 0 and 1. The result is adjusted to remain readable in the data display.

Formula​

uk=kK−1u_k = \frac{k}{K-1}uk​=K−1k​ Slope=K⋅K∑kukX[k]−∑kuk∑kX[k]∑kX[k](K∑kuk2−(∑kuk)2)Slope = K \cdot \frac{K\sum_k u_k X[k] - \sum_k u_k \sum_k X[k]} {\sum_k X[k]\left(K\sum_k u_k^2 - \left(\sum_k u_k\right)^2\right)}Slope=K⋅∑k​X[k](K∑k​uk2​−(∑k​uk​)2)K∑k​uk​X[k]−∑k​uk​∑k​X[k]​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> axe frequentiel normalise -> regression lineaire normalisee

Use in musical analysis​

It shows whether the spectrum tilts toward low or high frequencies. It can reveal filtering, changes in spectral density or prominent peaks in brightness.

Spectral Smoothness​

How it works​

Spectral Smoothness measures local spectral irregularity by comparing each bin with the logarithmic mean of its neighbors. A low value indicates a locally regular spectrum.

Formula​

Smoothness=1K−2∑k=1K−2∣log⁡(X[k]+ϵ)−log⁡(X[k−1]+ϵ)+log⁡(X[k+1]+ϵ)2∣Smoothness = \frac{1}{K-2} \sum_{k=1}^{K-2} \left| \log(X[k]+\epsilon) - \frac{\log(X[k-1]+\epsilon)+\log(X[k+1]+\epsilon)}{2} \right|Smoothness=K−21​k=1∑K−2​​log(X[k]+ϵ)−2log(X[k−1]+ϵ)+log(X[k+1]+ϵ)​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> log magnitudes -> comparaison locale aux voisins -> moyenne absolue

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to track spectral regularity or irregularity. It can help distinguish smooth, noisy, grainy or strongly resonant spectra.

Spectral Contrast​

How it works​

Spectral Contrast divides the spectrum into logarithmic bands and measures the relative difference between the highest and lowest value in each band. The result is the mean of the band contrasts.

Formula​

Contrastb=Peakb−ValleybPeakb+Valleyb+ϵContrast_b = \frac{Peak_b - Valley_b}{Peak_b + Valley_b + \epsilon}Contrastb​=Peakb​+Valleyb​+ϵPeakb​−Valleyb​​ Contrast=1B∑bContrastbContrast = \frac{1}{B}\sum_b Contrast_bContrast=B1​b∑​Contrastb​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> bandes logarithmiques -> peak/valley par bande -> moyenne

Use in musical analysis​

It can describe a spectrum’s clarity or internal contrast. In musical analysis, it can help identify changes in texture, density or separation between components.

Spectral Reflux​

How it works​

Spectral Reflux measures only magnitude increases between two consecutive spectra. Decreases in energy are ignored.

Formula​

Refluxt=∑kmax⁡(Xt[k]−Xt−1[k],0)2Reflux_t = \sqrt{\sum_k \max(X_t[k] - X_{t-1}[k], 0)^2}Refluxt​=k∑​max(Xt​[k]−Xt−1​[k],0)2​

Pipeline​

Fenetre precedente et courante -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> difference positive bin a bin -> norme euclidienne

Use in musical analysis​

It is useful for identifying emerging spectral components, attacks, component entries or sudden spectral enrichment. It can complement Spectral Flux in a segmentation interface.

Spectral Entropy​

How it works​

Spectral Entropy converts magnitudes into a probability distribution, then computes normalized Shannon entropy. A high value indicates energy spread over many bins.

Formula​

pk=X[k]∑jX[j]p_k = \frac{X[k]}{\sum_j X[j]}pk​=∑j​X[j]X[k]​ Entropy=−∑kpklog⁡2(pk)log⁡2(K)Entropy = -\frac{\sum_k p_k \log_2(p_k)}{\log_2(K)}Entropy=−log2​(K)∑k​pk​log2​(pk​)​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> FFT magnitudes lineaires -> normalisation en probabilites -> entropie de Shannon normalisee

Use in musical analysis​

It helps distinguish concentrated, pitched or resonant spectra from diffuse or noisy spectra. It is relevant to tracking changes in texture or density.

Harmonic Ratio​

How it works​

Harmonic Ratio measures signal periodicity through autocorrelation. It finds the highest normalized correlation for lags corresponding approximately to fundamental frequencies between 50 and 2000 Hz.

Formula​

HR=max⁡τ∈[τmin,τmax]∑nx[n]x[n+τ]∑nx[n]2HR = \max_{\tau \in [\tau_{min}, \tau_{max}]} \frac{\sum_n x[n]x[n+\tau]}{\sum_n x[n]^2}HR=τ∈[τmin​,τmax​]max​∑n​x[n]2∑n​x[n]x[n+τ]​ τmin=sr2000,τmax=sr50\tau_{min} = \frac{sr}{2000}, \quad \tau_{max} = \frac{sr}{50}τmin​=2000sr​,τmax​=50sr​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> autocorrelation normalisee -> maximum dans la plage de lags

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to track periodicity or the tonal character of a sound. It differs from an odd/even harmonics ratio, which compares even and odd harmonics after estimating partials.

Attack Time​

How it works​

Attack Time measures, within each frame, the time between reaching 10% and 90% of the local absolute maximum. This is a frame-based version, not an event-based measure relying on onset detection.

Formula​

t10=min⁡{n:∣x[n]∣≥0.1max⁡∣x∣}t_{10} = \min\{n : |x[n]| \geq 0.1 \max |x|\}t10​=min{n:∣x[n]∣≥0.1max∣x∣} t90=min⁡{n≥t10:∣x[n]∣≥0.9max⁡∣x∣}t_{90} = \min\{n \geq t_{10} : |x[n]| \geq 0.9 \max |x|\}t90​=min{n≥t10​:∣x[n]∣≥0.9max∣x∣} AttackTime=t90−t10srAttackTime = \frac{t_{90} - t_{10}}{sr}AttackTime=srt90​−t10​​

Pipeline​

Signal mono -> fenetre temporelle -> enveloppe absolue simple -> seuils 10% et 90% -> duree

Use in musical analysis​

It can provide a local indication of attack speed. For more robust analysis, a future version could measure attacks around detected events.

Onset Strength​

How it works​

Onset Strength measures the positive increase in RMS between the previous and current frames. It is therefore a simple energy-based onset strength measure.

Formula​

OSt=max⁡(RMSt−RMSt−1,0)OS_t = \max(RMS_t - RMS_{t-1}, 0)OSt​=max(RMSt​−RMSt−1​,0)

Pipeline​

Fenetre precedente et courante -> RMS temporel -> difference positive

Use in musical analysis​

It can help identify attacks or increases in energy. It is simpler than spectral methods, but can still be useful in a segmentation interface.

Stereo Balance​

How it works​

Stereo Balance measures the energy balance between the first two channels. The output is normalized for display: 0 means left, 0.5 center and 1 right.

Formula​

Bs=RMSR−RMSLRMSR+RMSLB_s = \frac{RMS_R - RMS_L}{RMS_R + RMS_L}Bs​=RMSR​+RMSL​RMSR​−RMSL​​ StereoBalance=Bs+12StereoBalance = \frac{B_s + 1}{2}StereoBalance=2Bs​+1​

Pipeline​

Canaux gauche/droit -> fenetres synchronisees -> RMS gauche et RMS droit -> balance signee -> normalisation 0...1

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to visualize left-right movement or imbalance. It does not measure stereo width, correlation or phase.

MID/SIDE Ratio​

How it works​

MID/SIDE Ratio measures the proportion of side energy in the stereo signal. A low value indicates a centered or mono signal; a high value indicates a wide or strongly lateral signal.

Formula​

Mid[n]=L[n]+R[n]2Mid[n] = \frac{L[n] + R[n]}{2}Mid[n]=2L[n]+R[n]​ Side[n]=R[n]−L[n]2Side[n] = \frac{R[n] - L[n]}{2}Side[n]=2R[n]−L[n]​ MSRatio=RMSSideRMSMid+RMSSideMSRatio = \frac{RMS_{Side}}{RMS_{Mid} + RMS_{Side}}MSRatio=RMSMid​+RMSSide​RMSSide​​

Pipeline​

Canaux gauche/droit -> transformation MID/SIDE -> RMS mid et side -> ratio side / total

Use in musical analysis​

Use it to track stereo width, spatialization effects and more or less centered passages. It complements Stereo Balance, which measures left-right position.
